Correlation vs Covariance in Statistics

1. Introduction
Correlation and covariance are statistical measures used to understand the relationship between two variables.

2. Covariance

Covariance measures the direction of the relationship between two variables.

Formula:

Cov(X, Y) = Σ[(Xi - X̄)(Yi - Ȳ)] / (n - 1)

Where:
- Xi = individual value of X
- Yi = individual value of Y
- X̄ = mean of X
- Ȳ = mean of Y
- n = number of observations

Interpretation:
- Positive covariance: X and Y tend to increase together.
- Negative covariance: when X increases, Y tends to decrease.
- Covariance close to zero: there may be little or no linear relationship.

Important limitation:
The magnitude of covariance depends on the units of the variables, so it is difficult to compare covariance values across different datasets.

3. Correlation

Correlation measures both the direction and strength of the linear relationship between two variables.

The Pearson correlation coefficient is:

r = Cov(X, Y) / (σX × σY)

Where:
- Cov(X, Y) = covariance between X and Y
- σX = standard deviation of X
- σY = standard deviation of Y

The correlation coefficient ranges from -1 to +1.

Interpretation:
- r = +1: perfect positive linear relationship.
- r = -1: perfect negative linear relationship.
- r = 0: no linear relationship.
- r close to +1: strong positive linear relationship.
- r close to -1: strong negative linear relationship.
- r close to 0: weak linear relationship.

4. Main Difference

Covariance:
- Shows the direction of the relationship.
- Has units based on the units of X and Y.
- Has no fixed range.
- Its magnitude is difficult to interpret directly.

Correlation:
- Shows both direction and strength.
- Is unitless.
- Always lies between -1 and +1.
- Is easier to interpret and compare.

5. Example

Suppose we have two variables:

X = Hours studied
Y = Exam score

If students who study more hours generally score higher, the covariance will tend to be positive and the correlation will also be positive.

If the correlation is 0.90, it indicates a strong positive linear relationship between hours studied and exam score.

6. Relationship Between Them

Correlation is essentially a standardized form of covariance.

Correlation = Covariance / (Standard deviation of X × Standard deviation of Y)

Therefore, correlation removes the effect of the units and scales the result to the range -1 to +1.

7. Covariance vs Correlation — Quick Comparison

Covariance:
- Direction: Yes
- Strength: Not directly standardized
- Range: No fixed range
- Units: Has units
- Easy to compare: No

Correlation:
- Direction: Yes
- Strength: Yes
- Range: -1 to +1
- Units: Unitless
- Easy to compare: Yes

8. Key Point to Remember

Covariance tells you whether two variables move together or in opposite directions.

Correlation tells you how strongly and in which direction two variables are linearly related.

Note:
Correlation does not necessarily imply causation. A strong correlation between two variables does not prove that one variable causes the other.
